## Q1. Foundations: LLM-based agent architectures and multi-agent systems (role specialisation, tool use, coordination)

### Takeaway
The surveys and papers checked describe LLM agents as LLMs that plan, use tools and act. A multi-agent system adds role profiling, communication paradigms (cooperative, debate, competitive) and communication structures (layered, decentralised, centralised, shared message pool). The surveys present multi-agent design as promising. They also name hallucination cascades, orchestration, scaling and evaluation as open problems.

### Cited Findings
- [F-REACT] Yao, Zhao, Yu, Du, Shafran, Narasimhan, Cao. "ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models." ICLR 2023 (oral; the page says "top 5% of papers"). arXiv 2210.03629. Single-agent design. The model interleaves reasoning traces with task-specific actions (for example, calls to a Wikipedia API). On ALFWorld it beats imitation and RL methods by 34% absolute success rate, and on WebShop by 10%, using only one or two in-context examples. On HotpotQA and FEVER, the paper says ReAct reduces the hallucination and error propagation seen in chain-of-thought (no numbers on the page). It also claims "improved human interpretability and trustworthiness over methods without reasoning or acting components." Status: VERIFIED-PAGE (ICLR virtual page), peer-reviewed. Supports 1.1 (tool use as the basis of agents) and 1.3 (traceable reasoning-action trajectories). — [ICLR 2023 oral page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12647); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)
- [F-GUO] Guo, Chen, Wang, Chang, Pei, Chawla, Wiest, Zhang. "Large Language Model Based Multi-agents: A Survey of Progress and Challenges." Proceedings of IJCAI-24, Survey Track, pp. 8048–8057. DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2024/890. The survey says the field moved from single-agent to multi-agent systems for complex problem solving and world simulation. It reviews agent profiling, communication and capability acquisition. Status: VERIFIED-PAGE and VERIFIED-FULLTEXT (sections 3.3 and 6, read from the IJCAI markdown version), peer-reviewed. — [IJCAI 2024](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0890); [full text](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/890.md)

  - Section 3.3, structures: layered (hierarchical roles); decentralised ("peer-to-peer network, where agents directly communicate with each other", used for example in debate); centralised ("a central agent coordinating the system's communication"); shared message pool (MetaGPT publish/subscribe). DOI 10.1007/s11704-024-40231-1. The survey proposes "a unified framework that encompasses a majority of the previous work" for building LLM agents, and reviews applications and evaluation. ### Inferences
- In Guo et al.'s taxonomy, the project's design (a deterministic orchestrator over a shared case state) sits between the "centralised" structure and the "shared message pool" structure. The difference is that the coordinator is a deterministic state machine, not an LLM agent. That difference is a design contribution the chapter can name explicitly.
- ReAct supports describing agent trajectories (thought, action, observation) as inspectable. That gives a basis for traceability claims.

## Q2. Multi-agent clinical decision support systems and their reported results

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed clinical multi-agent studies (MedAgents, MDAgents, Ke et al. in JMIR, Chen et al. in npj Digital Medicine) report gains over single-pass LLMs and simple prompting baselines. Nearly all of these are on exam-style QA or published case reports, use small samples, and depend on GPT-4. Interactive agent benchmarks (AgentClinic, MedAgentBench) show that sequential, tool-based clinical work is much harder than static QA. The one systematic review that could be verified (a preprint) found heavy use of synthetic data, single-centre designs, and no study that compares multi-agent with single-agent systems directly.

### Cited Findings
- [C-MEDAGENTS] Tang, Zou, Zhang, Li, Zhao, Zhang, Cohan, Gerstein. "MedAgents: Large Language Models as Collaborators for Zero-shot Medical Reasoning." Findings of ACL 2024, pp. 599–621. DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.33. Training-free, role-playing, multi-round discussion in five steps: gather domain experts, write individual analyses, summarise them into a report, iterate discussion until consensus, decide. Zero-shot evaluation on 9 datasets (MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA and 6 MMLU subtasks). Architecture: multi-agent, consensus-based, with a summariser step (partly centralised). Status: VERIFIED-PAGE, ABSTRACT-ONLY. No numeric results were read. Supports 1.1. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.33)
- [C-MDAGENTS] Kim, Park, Jeong, Chan, Xu, McDuff, Lee, Ghassemi, Breazeal, Park. "MDAgents: An Adaptive Collaboration of LLMs for Medical Decision-Making." NeurIPS 2024 (Advances in NeurIPS 37), Main Conference Track, pp. 79410–79452. DOI 10.52202/079017-2522. The search results also list a NeurIPS 2024 oral page. The framework assigns either solo or group collaboration depending on task complexity. It was best on 7 of 10 medical knowledge and multimodal benchmarks and improved on the previous best by up to 4.2% (p < 0.05). Moderator review plus external medical knowledge in group collaboration gave an 11.8% average accuracy gain. Its complexity classification was compared with physicians' ratings. Architecture: adaptive, with a moderator in group mode (centralised moderation). Status: VERIFIED-PAGE, ABSTRACT-ONLY, peer-reviewed. Supports 1.1 and 1.3. It also bears on Q3: the framework sometimes chooses a single agent for efficiency, which implies multi-agent is not always needed. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/90d1fc07f46e31387978b88e7e057a31-Abstract.html)
- [C-KE-JMIR] Correction to the brief: the Ke et al. paper is in JMIR, not npj Digital Medicine. Ke, Yang, Lie, Lim, Ning, Li, Abdullah, Ting, Liu. "Mitigating Cognitive Biases in Clinical Decision-Making Through Multi-Agent Conversations Using Large Language Models: Simulation Study." J Med Internet Res 2024;26:e59439. DOI 10.2196/59439, PMID 39561363, PMCID PMC11615553. The arXiv preprint title was "Enhancing Diagnostic Accuracy through Multi-Agent Conversations: Using LLMs to Mitigate Cognitive Bias" (arXiv 2401.14589). Status: VERIFIED-FULLTEXT (JMIR page), peer-reviewed. — [JMIR](https://jmir.org/2024/1/e59439)

    - Framework 3: two residents and a recorder, with one resident acting as devil's advocate against anchoring and confirmation bias.

  - Results: initial diagnosis was 0/80 correct in all frameworks. Final diagnosis accuracy was 51/80 (64%), 55/80 (69%) and 61/80 (76%) for Frameworks 3, 4 and 4-C. Humans scored 13/48 (27%) initially and 23/48 (48%) finally. Framework 4-C OR 3.49, p = .002; Framework 4 OR 2.39, p = .03; Framework 3 p = .10.

- [C-CHEN-NPJ] Chen, Yi, You, Liu, Wang, Li, Zhang, Guo, Fan, Chen, Lao, Fu, Li, Li. "Enhancing diagnostic capability with multi-agents conversational large language models." npj Digital Medicine 8, 159 (2025). DOI 10.1038/s41746-025-01550-0. Multi-Agent Conversation (MAC) framework modelled on multidisciplinary team (MDT) discussion, tested on 302 rare-disease cases with GPT-3.5, GPT-4 and MAC. MAC beat the single models in primary and follow-up consultations (diagnosis and suggested tests). Best configuration: four doctor agents plus a supervisor agent, with GPT-4 as the base model. Results were consistent across repeated runs. MAC also beat Chain-of-Thought, Self-Refine and Self-Consistency "with higher performance and more output tokens." Architecture: multi-agent with a supervisor (partly centralised). Status: ABSTRACT-ONLY (abstract rebuilt from the OpenAlex inverted index), peer-reviewed. Exact accuracy figures were not read. Supports 1.1 and 1.3. The token-cost caveat is relevant to Q3. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1038%2FS41746-025-01550-0); [DOAJ record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-3cc16be4a38d444ca1505730e1c45eaf)
- [C-AGENTCLINIC] Schmidgall, Ziaei, Harris, Reis, Jopling, Moor. "AgentClinic: a multimodal agent benchmark to evaluate AI in simulated clinical environments." arXiv 2405.07960 (v1 2024-05-13; v5 2025-05-25). ### Inferences
- Taken together, the clinical multi-agent literature mostly compares against single-pass LLMs or simple prompting (CoT, Self-Consistency, Self-Refine). It rarely compares against a strong single agent with tools at equal compute. The Gorenshtein review says so directly ("no study provides a comparison of multi-agent to single-agent"). This is a defensible problem-statement point for 1.2.
- In the Gorenshtein preprint, the median gain over baseline is larger for single-agent tool calling (+53%) than for multi-agent designs (+14% and +17%). These are medians across different tasks, not head-to-head comparisons, so they cannot show that single agents are better. They do mean the review gives no support for claiming multi-agent superiority.
- Ke et al. (devil's advocate and bias-aware senior role) gives direct, peer-reviewed motivation for the project's critic agent. Its 16-case sample and use of published cases limit how far the result generalises.

## Q3. Do multi-agent gains persist against strong or compute-matched single-agent baselines? Failure modes and overheads

### Takeaway
The balance of evidence is mixed to sceptical. Peer-reviewed work shows strongly prompted single agents nearly match multi-agent discussion (ACL 2024), and shows that multi-agent debate does not reliably beat self-consistency or ensembling (ICML 2024). A medical benchmark finds multi-agent collaboration does not consistently beat advanced single LLMs. Recent preprints document 14 failure modes and show that under fixed compute, multi-agent overhead hurts tool-heavy and sequential tasks. Gains are conditional, not automatic.

### Cited Findings
- [N-WANG-ACL] Wang, Wang, Su, Tong, Song. "Rethinking the Bounds of LLM Reasoning: Are Multi-Agent Discussions the Key?" ACL 2024 (Long Papers), pp. 6106–6131. DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.331. Across many reasoning tasks and backbones, "a single-agent LLM with strong prompts can achieve almost the same best performance" as the best discussion method. Multi-agent discussion wins only when the prompt contains no demonstrations. Status: VERIFIED-PAGE, ABSTRACT-ONLY, peer-reviewed. The tasks are general reasoning, not clinical. Supports 1.2. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331)
- [N-SMIT-ICML] Smit, Grinsztajn, Duckworth, Barrett, Pretorius. "Should we be going MAD? A Look at Multi-Agent Debate Strategies for LLMs." ICML 2024, PMLR 235: 45883–45905. "Multi-agent debating systems, in their current form, do not reliably outperform other proposed prompting strategies, such as self-consistency and ensembling." MAD is more sensitive to hyperparameters. Tuning agreement levels can let it beat the non-debate protocols tested. The paper studies cost, time and accuracy trade-offs. Status: VERIFIED-PAGE, ABSTRACT-ONLY, peer-reviewed. An earlier arXiv title, seen only as a search-result title, was "Are we going MAD? Benchmarking Multi-Agent Debate between Language Models for Medical Q&A." That suggests medical QA datasets, but the PMLR page did not confirm it. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)
- [N-MEDAGENTBOARD] Zhu, He, Hu, Zheng, Zhang, Wang, Gao, Ma, Yu. "MedAgentBoard: Benchmarking Multi-Agent Collaboration with Conventional Methods for Diverse Medical Tasks." arXiv 2505.12371 (v2 2025-10-30). Covers 4 task types: medical (visual) QA, lay summaries, structured-EHR prediction, and clinical workflow automation. Multi-agent collaboration "does not consistently outperform advanced single LLMs" (for example, on textual medical QA). Conventional specialised methods "generally maintain better performance" on medical VQA and EHR prediction. Multi-agent helps in some cases, such as task completeness in workflow automation. Status: the arXiv page shows no venue. A NeurIPS 2025 poster page (neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121792) appeared in search results but was not opened, so the venue is likely but unconfirmed. Supports 1.2. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12371v2); [NeurIPS 2025 listing (not opened)](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121792)
- [N-MAST] Cemri, Pan, Yang, Agrawal, Chopra, Tiwari, Keutzer, Parameswaran, Klein, Ramchandran, Zaharia, Gonzalez, Stoica. "Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?" arXiv 2503.13657 (v1 2025-03-17; v3 2025-10-26, comment "ArXiv v3").
  - MAST taxonomy: 14 failure modes in 3 categories (system design issues, inter-agent misalignment, task verification), built from 150 traces with expert inter-annotator kappa 0.88.
  - MAST-Data: more than 1,600 annotated traces from 7 MAS frameworks (coding, math and general agent tasks), plus an LLM-as-judge annotator.
  - The authors argue MAS gains on popular benchmarks "are often minimal." The Berkeley project page (earlier version) says ChatDev achieves "only 33.33% correctness" on ProgramDev.
  - Status: PREPRINT. Neither the arXiv page nor the project page lists a peer-reviewed venue. The tasks are non-clinical.
  - Supports 1.2 (coordination and verification failures) and 1.3 (justifies a deterministic orchestrator and an explicit verifier). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657v3); [project page](https://sky.cs.berkeley.edu/project/mast/)
- [N-SCALING] Kim, Gu, Park, Park, Schmidgall, Heydari, Yan, Zhang, Zhuang, Malhotra, Liang, Park, Yang, Xu, Du, Patel, Althoff, McDuff, Liu. "Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems." arXiv 2512.08296 (v1 2025-12-09; v2 2025-12-17; v3 2026-04-08 exists but was not read). Status: PREPRINT. Non-clinical benchmarks.
  - Setup: 180 configurations; 4 benchmarks (Finance-Agent, BrowseComp-Plus, PlanCraft, Workbench); 5 architectures (single-agent, plus independent, centralised, decentralised and hybrid multi-agent); 3 LLM families.
  - Under fixed compute budgets, multi-agent overhead disproportionately hurts tool-heavy tasks.
  - Coordination gives diminishing or negative returns once single-agent baselines exceed about 45%.
  - On sequential reasoning tasks, every multi-agent variant degraded performance by 39–70%.
  - Error amplification is 17.2× for independent agents and 4.4× under centralised coordination.
  - Centralised coordination improved parallelisable tasks by 80.8%. Decentralised coordination did best on web navigation (+9.2% vs +0.2%).
  - Supports 1.2 and 1.3. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v2)
- [C-CHEN-NPJ, cost note] Chen et al.'s MAC beat CoT, Self-Refine and Self-Consistency "with higher performance and more output tokens," so the comparison was not compute-matched. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1038%2FS41746-025-01550-0)
- [C-MDAGENTS, efficiency note] MDAgents routes simpler cases to a single agent to "balance efficiency and accuracy." The design itself treats multi-agent collaboration as costly and not always needed. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/90d1fc07f46e31387978b88e7e057a31-Abstract.html)
- [SR-GORENSHTEIN, overhead note] Cost and runtime were seldom reported in the included clinical agent studies, and latency was not reported. — [medRxiv PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.22.25334232.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- The scaling result (sequential reasoning degraded by 39–70%; tool-heavy tasks hurt under fixed compute) is relevant because clinical work-ups are sequential. AgentClinic shows that clinical tasks become much harder in sequential form. This combination motivates a bounded pipeline (fixed stages, one bounded revision) rather than open-ended agent conversation. These are non-clinical preprints, so frame it as motivation, not proof.
- The evidence supports a narrow framing: specialised roles (critic, verifier) may help with specific failure types, such as anchoring and unverified claims. Whether the full pipeline beats a strong single agent with the same tools and budget has to be tested. The project should include a compute-matched single-agent baseline.

### Gaps
- No clinical study found here gives a compute-matched (token-matched or cost-matched) multi-agent versus single-agent-with-tools comparison. The Gorenshtein review confirms none among its 20 included studies.
- Latency and cost figures for clinical multi-agent systems were not found in peer-reviewed sources.
- The peer-review status of MAST (NeurIPS 2025?) and MedAgentBoard (NeurIPS 2025 poster listing) needs final confirmation from the proceedings.

## Q4. Centralised versus decentralised coordination, and auditability and traceability

### Takeaway
Guo et al. (IJCAI 2024) define both structures but give no verdict. The only quantitative comparison found is a non-clinical preprint showing that centralised coordination contains error amplification (4.4× vs 17.2× for independent agents), while decentralised coordination does better on some exploratory tasks. Sources tie traceability to explicit reasoning and action traces (ReAct) and call for controlling information flow between agents (Guo §6.2). No source found here shows that LLM orchestration itself is auditable.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions of decentralised (peer-to-peer) and centralised (central coordinating agent) structures. Debate systems such as Du et al. 2023 are classed as decentralised. — [Guo et al., IJCAI 2024](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/890.md)
- "One agent's hallucination can have a cascading effect"; the survey calls for controlling information flow between agents. — [Guo et al., IJCAI 2024](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/890.md)
- Centralised coordination limits error amplification to 4.4× (vs 17.2× independent) and helps parallelisable tasks (+80.8%). Decentralised coordination is better for web navigation (+9.2% vs +0.2%). (Preprint.) — [Kim et al. 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v2)
- Two of MAST's three failure categories are "inter-agent misalignment" and "task verification." (Preprint.) — [Cemri et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657v3)
- ReAct trajectories are claimed to improve "human interpretability and trustworthiness." — [ReAct, ICLR 2023](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12647)
- The clinical systems with the strongest results use a moderator or supervisor: MDAgents (moderator review plus external knowledge, +11.8% average) and MAC (supervisor plus four doctor agents was optimal). — [MDAgents](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/90d1fc07f46e31387978b88e7e057a31-Abstract.html); [Chen et al. 2025](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1038%2FS41746-025-01550-0)
- The systematic review recommends human-in-the-loop oversight. — [Gorenshtein et al., medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.22.25334232.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- A deterministic state-machine orchestrator is a stronger form of centralisation than an LLM moderator or supervisor. Every transition can be replayed and logged. The sources support centralisation for containing errors, but they do not test deterministic orchestration specifically. Present it as the project's design hypothesis.
- Physician final authority fits the review's human-in-the-loop recommendation and the "aid, not replacement" framing in Ke et al.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed clinical study comparing centralised and decentralised coordination was found.
- No source on formal audit logging or regulatory traceability for clinical LLM agents was retrieved. The parallel regulatory and safety research may cover this.

## Q5. Claim-to-source list for Chapter 1 (supported, conditional, and NOT supported)

### Takeaway
Chapter 1 can safely say that multi-agent designs are an active, promising direction with peer-reviewed gains over single-pass LLMs. It should not say that multi-agent designs are generally better than strong single agents, or that they reduce hallucination automatically.

### Cited Findings
Supported (peer-reviewed):
- S1. LLM agents combine reasoning with tool use and actions, which improves interactive decision-making. (1.1) — [ReAct](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12647)
- S2. LLM-based MAS use role profiling and communication paradigms and structures, including centralised and decentralised ones. (1.1) — [Guo et al.](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/890.md)
- S3. Role-based multi-agent collaboration has been applied to medical QA (9 datasets, zero-shot). (1.1) — [MedAgents](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.33)
- S4. Adaptive, complexity-aware collaboration was best on 7 of 10 medical benchmarks (up to +4.2%). (1.1, 1.3) — [MDAgents](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/90d1fc07f46e31387978b88e7e057a31-Abstract.html)
- S5. A multi-agent conversation with a bias-aware role reached 76% final-diagnosis accuracy, against 48% for three physicians, on 16 cases. The initial single-pass diagnosis was 0% correct. (1.3, critic role) — [Ke et al., JMIR 2024](https://jmir.org/2024/1/e59439)
- S6. An MDT-style MAC beat single GPT-4 and CoT, Self-Refine and Self-Consistency on 302 rare-disease cases, but used more output tokens. (1.1, 1.3) — [Chen et al., npj Digit Med 2025](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1038%2FS41746-025-01550-0)
- S7. Strongly prompted single agents nearly match multi-agent discussion on reasoning tasks. (1.2) — [Wang et al., ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331)
- S8. Multi-agent debate does not reliably beat self-consistency or ensembling, and is sensitive to hyperparameters. (1.2) — [Smit et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html)
- S9. In multi-agent systems, one agent's hallucination can cascade. (1.2) — [Guo et al.](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/890.md)

Supported, preprint only (label as preprint in the chapter):
- P1. Clinical agent studies are mostly synthetic-data and single-centre (65% synthetic; 20/20 single-centre), and none compares multi-agent with single-agent directly. (1.2) — [Gorenshtein et al., medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.22.25334232.full.pdf)
- P2. Sequential, interactive diagnosis can cut accuracy to below one tenth of static QA. (1.2) — [AgentClinic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07960)
- P3. The best LLM reached only 69.67% success on realistic EHR agent tasks. (1.2) — [MedAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14654)
- P4. Multi-agent systems show 14 failure modes across system design, inter-agent misalignment and verification. (1.2, 1.3) — [MAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657v3)
- P5. Multi-agent collaboration does not consistently beat advanced single LLMs on medical tasks. (1.2) — [MedAgentBoard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12371v2)
- P6. Centralised coordination limits error amplification (4.4× vs 17.2×). Multi-agent variants degrade sequential reasoning by 39–70%, and overhead hurts tool-heavy tasks under fixed compute. (1.2, 1.3) — [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v2)

NOT supported by the sources reviewed (do not assert these):
- X1. "Multi-agent collaboration automatically reduces hallucination." Not found. Guo et al. warn that hallucinations can cascade, and MAST lists verification failures. The only verified hallucination-reduction claim is ReAct's: a single agent using a retrieval tool, compared with CoT. — [Guo et al.](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/890.md); [ReAct](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12647)
- X2. "Multi-agent systems outperform single agents in clinical decision support." No head-to-head clinical evidence against strong or tool-using single agents was found; the review says none exists in its sample. The available evidence is mixed. — [Gorenshtein et al.](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.22.25334232.full.pdf); [MedAgentBoard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12371v2); [Wang et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.331)
- X3. "More agents means better performance." Contradicted. The review reports an inverted-U pattern (best at 4–5 agents, with heavy caveats). Ke et al.'s fifth agent did not participate effectively. The scaling preprint shows negative returns once the single-agent baseline exceeds about 45%. — [Gorenshtein et al.](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.22.25334232.full.pdf); [Ke et al.](https://jmir.org/2024/1/e59439); [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v2)
- X4. "Multi-agent clinical systems have been validated in real-world clinical practice." Not supported. Real patient data appeared in only 2 of the 20 included studies (117 patients). — [Gorenshtein et al.](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.22.25334232.full.pdf)
- X5. "Multi-agent gains are compute-neutral or cost-free." Not supported. MAC used more output tokens, cost and latency were rarely reported, and overhead hurts tool-heavy tasks at fixed compute (preprint). — [Chen et al.](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1038%2FS41746-025-01550-0); [Gorenshtein et al.](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.22.25334232.full.pdf); [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v2)
- X6. "LLM-to-LLM orchestration is inherently auditable." No supporting source was found.

### Inferences
- Suggested framing for 1.2: "Multi-agent LLM designs report gains over single-pass LLMs on medical QA and case-based diagnosis. Evidence that these gains persist against strong or compute-matched single-agent baselines is limited and mixed, and documented coordination and verification failures motivate controlled, auditable orchestration."
